# Decisions

Each decision is recorded as written in the build brief, with a one-line reason and the alternative we rejected.

| ID | Decision | Why | Alternative rejected |
|----|----------|-----|----------------------|
| D01 | Gemini extracts facts into a fixed JSON schema. Code validates and scores. Gemini never decides a score or a band. | Scores must be repeatable, testable and explainable line by line. | Asking Gemini to rate each account 1 to 10 directly. |
| D02 | One accounts table, three column-owner groups: Arjun-owned, CRM-owned, system-owned. The system can only write system-owned columns. Enforced in code, in the database, and by test. | Arjun's notes are his work; one guarded table keeps one source of truth without ever overwriting them. | Separate tables per writer joined at read time. |
| D03 | Scores are recomputed from events on every run, with time decay. Quiet accounts fall automatically. Nothing is stored as a permanent "hot" flag. | A trigger loses value with time; recomputing removes stale "hot" accounts without manual cleanup. | A sticky hot flag cleared by hand. |
| D04 | Fit layer: account size, sector, frontline and multi-site share, India footprint. Size band peaks at 50 to 5,000 employees with a quiet taper above. Never state any "product breaks" reasoning anywhere. | Fit is a mild tiebreaker, not a filter, and must stay neutral in tone. | Hard exclusion of accounts outside the size band. |
| D05 | Relationship-aware reading. Prospects get outreach opportunities. Signed customers get an account-health lane: a buying-type signal at a customer is a churn risk (retention play), growth signals are expansion, no contact for 30+ days is re-engagement. | The same news means different things for a prospect and a customer. | One lane for every account regardless of stage. |
| D06 | Hot List window is "since the last send, minimum 3 days". This covers the Thursday to Monday gap that a fixed 3-day window would lose. The brief says last three days; this is a deliberate superset. | Thursday to Monday is 4 days; a fixed 3-day window drops Thursday's signals. | A fixed 3-day window. |
| D07 | LinkedIn posts are not scraped. Coverage of LinkedIn-type information comes through news and company pages only. | LinkedIn terms forbid scraping and it carries personal data risk. | Scraping LinkedIn posts or using a grey-market API. |
| D08 | HubSpot is in the build. If the token is missing or the API fails, the app runs on seed data and shows a banner. | The CRM link is part of the brief, but the demo must never depend on a third party being up. | Making HubSpot a hard dependency. |
| D09 | People are shown only from cited public sources, each with role, source URL and as-of date. No guessed emails or phones. "No verified contact found" is a valid result. | Guessed contacts are wrong often and raise privacy concerns (DPDP Act). | Email pattern guessing or enrichment tools. |
| D10 | Scheduling uses Vercel Cron, one ingest job and one morning job, daily. The morning job also sends the Hot List on Monday and Thursday. | Vercel Hobby allows daily cron jobs; two jobs keep ingest and send apart. | An external scheduler or a cron job per weekday. |
| D11 | Two different outputs: the Hot List "outreach angle" (one line, uses approved product facts only, per the brief) and the dashboard "soft-touch draft" (engagement only, no pitch, no product claims, per Arjun's preference). | The brief asks for an angle; Arjun wants first touches without a pitch. | One combined pitch email. |
| D12 | No auto-sending of anything to prospects, ever. Only emails to Arjun are automated. | A wrong automated email to a prospect is costly and cannot be undone. | Auto-sending drafts on a schedule. |
| D13 | Source tiers: T1 company site, filings, regulator, exchange announcements. T2 established business press and HR trade press, publication name always shown. Everything else is stored as context only and is never scored. | Scores must rest on sources Arjun would trust in front of a buyer. | Scoring any site the news feed returns. |
| D14 | Score scale is 1 to 10 (Hot 8 to 10, Warm 5 to 7, Cold 1 to 4) with a full explanation panel, not a bare number. | A small scale is easy to read; the panel shows exactly how the number was built. | A 0 to 100 score with no breakdown. |

## Build-time decisions (added during the build)

| ID | Decision | Why | Alternative rejected |
|----|----------|-----|----------------------|
| B01 | The Components Map is generated from a small script (scripts/render-map.mjs) into SVG, then PNG. | Same source for v1 and v2, easy to diff. | Drawing by hand in a GUI tool. |
| B02 | Next.js Cache Components is turned off. | Every page is per-user and behind sign-in, so nothing benefits from static caching. | Keeping it on and wrapping every read in Suspense. |
| B03 | LEADERSHIP_CHANGE half-life tuned from 10 to 14 days. | With 10 days a 14-day-old leadership change scores 4, but the brief requires 5 or 6. With 14 days: fresh scores 8, 14 days old scores 5. | Raising fit points, which would lift every account. |
| B04 | Server code talks to Postgres through one small SQL interface: pg in production, PGlite (in-process Postgres) in tests and as a seed fallback. | The real migration, write-guard trigger included, runs in CI with no external database. | Mocking the database in tests. |
| B05 | The Next.js agent-rules block at the end of AGENTS.md is kept as generated, even though it contains em dashes. | `next dev` writes and re-adds this block itself; editing it would only create churn. All text we write follows the no-em-dash rule. | Hand-editing a tool-generated block. |
| B06 | Vercel functions are pinned to Tokyo (hnd1). | The Supabase database is in ap-northeast-1 (Tokyo); from Washington (iad1) every query crossed the Pacific and a live ingest took 4 min 47 s. | Moving the database, which would need a new project. |
| B07 | Test build: with SM_TEST_MODE=true, any email and passcode sign in as the demo user, and a TEST BUILD badge shows on every page. | The user asked for an open test build for evaluators. The server signs in with the stored demo credentials, so Supabase sessions, route protection and sign-out still work; one env var turns it off. | Disabling auth entirely. |
| B08 | The Hot List goes out every morning (supersedes the Monday and Thursday schedule in D06 and D10). Each list has new accounts, updates on accounts already sent (articles found after the previous send) and a still-on-the-list line. Each item includes why now (Gemini, verified fields only), all supporting articles with quotes, and a HubSpot brief. | Arjun asked for a daily list with follow-ups and enough context to act without opening other tools. | Keeping Monday and Thursday and adding a separate update email. |
| B09 | Brand: colours taken from keka.com (primary #7c46f1, deep purple #5412ae for links, lavender #ABA5FF for dark-mode links, tint #F2F0FA). Body font Nunito Sans as on keka.com; headings stay Sora because Keka uses TT Commons Pro, a licensed font. | The user asked for exact Keka colours; all pairs checked at 5.25:1 or better. | Embedding a licensed font without a licence. |
| B10 | New trigger type VENDOR_PARTNERSHIP (base 6, half-life 10 days): the company signs a tech, ERP, HR, payroll or workforce services vendor or implementation partner. At a prospect it is outreach; at a customer it is expansion, or churn risk when the vendor is an HR or payroll provider. | The user asked for a partner angle; a new vendor often means systems are being re-chosen. | Treating every partnership as a buying signal. |
| B11 | Login is open access for evaluation: no passcode field; the server signs in as the demo user. The TEST BUILD badge was removed so the app looks live. | The user asked for a live-looking app that evaluators can enter without a password. | A visible test-mode badge on every page. |
| B12 | Flag accounts above 5,000 employees as "Above ICP size". Keka's 77 published customer stories are mostly 50 to 3,000 employees and Indian mid-market. | Arjun can see at a glance that a large account is a stretch; scoring already tapers fit above 5,000. | Dropping large accounts, which would hide real triggers. |
| B13 | Fit follows Keka segments: SMB under 100, mid-market (MM) 100 to 500, enterprise (ENT) 500+. Arjun is on the Enterprise Sales Team, so 500 to 5,000 scores full fit, above 5,000 tapers only to 0.7, MM 0.6, SMB 0.3. Every account shows an SMB, MM or ENT tag; MM and SMB say to route to that team. Replaces B12. | Segment definitions given by the user (Keka). | The brief's 50 to 5,000 band, which treated mid-market as core for an enterprise team. |
| B14 | Account history is shown only where it is real: (1) verified signals 15 to 90 days old, from the same pipeline, labelled "context, not scored"; (2) a score-over-time chart computed with the real formula from verified events (past 14 days, dashed 14-day projection), labelled as computed; (3) sm_signal_history in HubSpot. No further invented CRM history. | The user wanted context that holds up in the viva. Each item is traceable to sources or to the formula. | Generating months of demo activity, which would weaken the "everything is cited" claim. |
| B15 | Motion with GSAP (@gsap/react): stat cards rise and count up, list rows cascade on filter, detail sections and score badge animate in, the trend line draws. Spotlight hover glow on stat cards (pattern from 21st.dev, rebuilt in CSS). Opacity and transform only, under 1 s, all off for prefers-reduced-motion. | The user asked for interactivity; motion stays subtle so it helps the Design score without layout shift. | Installing 21st.dev shadcn components (would add shadcn/ui and framer-motion hours before the deadline). |
| B16 | The ingest resumes from the account where the last run stopped (runs.counts.resume_from), and "Data may be stale" is shown only when the last ingest failed, never ran or is over 26 hours old. A run stopped by its time limit or caps gets a coverage note ("checked 40 of 73 accounts"). One account's unreachable feed is logged, not a partial run. | Accounts were scanned alphabetically and the 4-minute budget ran out around account 40 every day, so the same 33 accounts were never checked, and every email said the data might be stale even though it was hours old. | A longer budget alone (still a fixed tail if the account list grows) or hiding partial runs (dishonest). |
| B17 | Project on hold: every page shows "This project is on hold. Please contact ADMIN.", every API (crons included) returns 503, and the Vercel crons are removed. The switch is on unless SM_ON_HOLD=false. | The assignment is submitted and assessed; the user asked to stop everything except the Vercel site. Two layers (no cron schedule, and 503 in the proxy) mean nothing runs even if a cron is called. | Deleting the Vercel project, which would take the link down. |
